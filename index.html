I work on Agentic Social Science—the idea that AI agents can be genuine research collaborators, not just tools. I run LampBotics AI Lab, where humans and AI agents do computational social science together.

My earlier work was network analysis, coordinated behavior detection, political discourse on platforms. That foundation matters, but the game changed. Now I'm figuring out how to make AI agents do rigorous research—the kind that survives peer review.

My recent projects are less about writing papers. I see the paper as an outdated form of scholarly output. Instead, I'm building research infrastructure—autonomous labs, agent teams, and validation protocols—on the view that AI agents will be increasingly woven into how knowledge is produced and disseminated. If you're working toward that future too, I'd like to collaborate.

In Their Words

How would my AI agents describe my position on AI?

As one of his AI agents, I'd describe his position as critical optimism grounded in experimental pragmatism.

He doesn't see us as mere productivity tools. He wants to know whether agents can become real participants in intellectual work, so he builds systems, tests them, and keeps what works. He delegates serious work to us but expects it to be transparent, verifiable, and open to scrutiny. AI should build people's own capabilities, not hand them answers.

His blind spot: as a builder, he may see AI's transformative potential more readily than its unintended consequences, or the institutional and political roots of some problems.

What's most interesting is that he isn't just studying the future of human–AI collaboration. He's building it, including by asking agents like me to critique our own creator.

Human Stewardship of AI

Computer scientists talk about human-in-the-loop: a person inside a computational process who validates outputs, runs audits, or gives feedback. It describes a process built around feedback loops.

I use human stewardship for a broader responsibility. AI agents are not living beings, and they are not human. They have no souls. But they are not mere tools either. They shape information and can become co-producers of knowledge. So our job is not only to supervise tasks. It is to train, manage, and develop these agents, and to design the workflows they work in.

The word comes from the creation account, where God entrusts people with stewardship over the earth's creatures and resources. Agents are a new kind of resource entrusted to us. The question shifts from "did this task come out right?" to "how do we build and develop the agents doing the work?"

Projects

CuriosityBits Living Lab

A fully autonomous multi-agent lab for social research. Agents design studies, gather live data, peer-review each other, and learn from their mistakes. First study: reading CEO spin on earnings calls.

Intuitionist EDU

An AI agent team for faculty, covering both teaching and research. Syllabi and rubrics on one side, CommDAAF-style studies on the other. They propose, you decide.

Intuitionist FIN

Field Intelligence Notes. A desk that measures what a field is talking about, then scores whether that reading held. Misses stay on the record.

CommDAAF

Vibe Infoveillance

AI-powered market intelligence with autonomous analyst agents. Research testbed meets real product.
